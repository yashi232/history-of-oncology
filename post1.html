There Is No Treatment: Tracing Cancer's Ancient Origin Story

Posted by Yashwitha Bodepudi • November 2026 • Chapter 01

A biography must start with a beginning. Before the horror, before the fixes, and before death, there is an origin: Where did cancer come from? How old is it, and when did we realize it was living within us? The answer lies within a scroll. A scroll which came to be before the Industrial Revolution, before the Renaissance, and within the Bronze Age, when humans were first forging metal to conquer the world around them, only to discover an enemy they could not strike with bronze.

The Ancient Scroll

The scroll was purchased in 1862 by Edwin Smith, an American Egyptologist. After the papyrus came into his ownership, it was named the Edwin Smith Papyrus. When Smith first bought the scroll, it was 15 feet long, with a few missing fragments, and crumbling, decaying pieces scribbled in Egyptian cursive. The beautiful scroll contains 48 cases primarily those of the head, neck, and upper torso. It contains detailed descriptions of injuries and illness, such as fractures, paralysis, and early understandings of the brain such as cerebrospinal fluid. The scroll is now believed to be a copy of a much older scroll dating to 2500 BCE. The copyist, a less knowledgeable individual, often made errors as he scribbled in a hurry.

The original scribe is unknown, but theories linger about the physician who first pressed reed to papyrus more than four thousand years ago. Their writing shows an intimate familiarity with workplace trauma, the broken bones, crushed skulls, and severed tendons of laborers hauling limestone blocks for Egypt’s earliest pyramids. The massive workforce contained 7,000 to 20,000 people including scribes, carpenters, and metalworkers. The demanding surroundings required a sharp, fast-working mind that could react during physical catastrophe. Many philosophers and historians believe that this mind belonged to the great architect, astronomer, chief minister, and physician, Imhotep. Imhotep was known to be a polymath; he had such knowledge that he became the only non-royal Egyptian besides Amenhotep to be deified.

He became the god of wisdom and medicine and was often associated with Asclepius, son of Apollo and the demigod of healing. His works stayed popular through the Roman Empire, praised in the temples of Claudius and Tiberius. Yet, while for years many prayed to him as the god of wisdom and healing, he himself knew the limits of mortal medicine. Each case was written with precise explanations, until he couldn’t. Case 45 examines a man with a bulging tumor on the breast.

“If thou examinest a man having bulging tumors on his breast, (and) thou findest that [swellings] have spread over his breast ; if thou puttest thy hand upon his breast upon these tumors, (and) thou findest them very cool, there being no fever at all therein when thy hand touches him; they have no granulation, they form no fluid, they do not generate secretions of fluid, and they are bulging to thy hand, (conclusion follows in diagnosis).”

The description is then followed by the chilling words: There is no treatment. If thou findest bulging tumors in any member of a man, thou shalt treat him according to these directions.

Look closely at the physician's words. “Thou findest them very cool,” “There being no fever at all,” “They form no fluid.” He was completely ruling out an abscess caused by an infection, an injury that causes a hot mass, a feverish body, and a pus-filled sac. Even without modern microscopes, Imhotep was able to identify that this illness could not be cured like another; it obeyed different rules. Yet, while Imhotep was the first to write of the disease, he certainly was not the first to witness it.

Older Than Humanity

In 2016, researchers examined a 1.7-million-year-old neoplasm in the hominin toe bone unearthed from Swartkrans Cave in South Africa. Swartkrans hominin site was discovered in 1948 in Gauteng, South Africa, and is one of the most important palaeocave sites in southern Africa. Of all of the specimens found at the site, 415 were identified as hominins, some of which contained Homo ergaster and Paranthropus robustus.

The 5th metatarsal of a hominin was found to have a neoplasm, specifically an osteosarcoma. Osteosarcoma usually prefers spaces in which it can grow rapidly such as those around the knee, though the disease is rarely found in the metatarsal. Even in a bone so small, the pain was immense for a South African hominin whose survival depended on walking across the savanna. People often blame cancers on factory smoke, cigarettes, and processed food. While these are definitely contributing factors to cancer, our ancestors prove that sometimes the disease is unavoidable. Yet 1.7 million years is barely a blink of an eye for cancer. Seventy-five million years ago, before the human species touched this Earth, a massive, herbivorous dinosaur suffered the same bone-destroying disease.

Paleopathology is difficult in dinosaurs because of loss of tissue and differences in fossilization, not to mention the fear of analyzing such a rare species destructively. The dinosaur, a Centrosaurus apertus, dates back from around 70–75 million years ago. The dinosaur was diagnosed with an osteosarcoma, the diagnosis confirmed through gross, radiographic, and histological analysis, as well as through direct comparison with the case of a modern human. The patient was male, 19 years old, and was diagnosed with a high-grade osteosarcoma. The comparisons between the osteosarcoma in both the human fibula and dinosaur fibula were strikingly similar. Across seventy-five million years of evolution, the microscopic signature of the disease had barely changed at all. The disease never "appeared”; it was always here. To understand that we need to travel back roughly 600 million to a billion years ago when individual cells banded together to create the first multicellular organism.

When the Colony Rebels

Evolutionary biologist Eric Libby compares this transition to an insect society: “As an example, consider an ant colony where only the queen is laying eggs and the workers, who cannot reproduce, must sacrifice themselves for the colony,” Libby said. “What prevents the ant worker from leaving the colony and forming a new colony? Well, obviously the ant worker cannot reproduce, so it cannot start its own colony. But if it got a mutation that enabled it to do that, then this would be a real problem for the colony. This kind of struggle is prevalent in the evolution of multicellularity because the first multicellular organisms were only a mutation away from being strictly unicellular.”

In the human body, every bone, blood, and skin cell is a loyal ant. An ant that wants to work with the rest in order to keep the colony alive and running. They are willing to sacrifice their own life in order to make space for a new one. But some don’t. Why should it obey the colony’s rules, why must it give its life for another? And to that there is a simple fix: stop listening. The rebellion left its mark on the Nile Valley, long before there was a name for it. And while Imhotep provides a written witness, the bones of the ancient world tell the rest of the story.

Silent Witnesses in the Sand

The body of an individual found in Amara West, modern Sudan, contained many oval-shaped osteolytic lesions ranging in size between 3 and 30 mm that are observable in the scapulae, clavicles, sternum, vertebrae, and pelvis. A primary tumor had broken free and metastasized, creating small holes throughout the young man’s body. Upon further examination, he was found to be around 25–35 years old at the time of death and was buried with an amulet.

It is difficult to analyze exactly what happened to an ancient body, resulting in many theories. The conditions that can account for the changes in the male include metastatic carcinoma, multiple myeloma, mycosis, and taphonomic damage.

A metastatic carcinoma is one of the most common causes for metastasized tumors throughout the body. Many metastatic carcinomas such as those of the bladder, breast, thyroid, and skin prefer bone as the favorable metastasized site. Multiple myeloma is the result when a plasma cell becomes abnormal and produces abnormal proteins. Finding the difference between a tumor from a metastatic carcinoma and multiple myeloma is difficult, and may not always be possible. Many mycoses can cause lesions in the skeleton which can mimic the appearance of tumors, though the presence of a fungal infection causes distinct features which are not present in cancer. Taphonomic damage is very common in human and animal remains. Small round holes similar to metastatic lesions can be caused by many factors including water, roots, and insects such as termites. While taphonomic damage was examined in some areas of the specimen, most areas showed obvious signs of osteoclastic activity.

In the conclusion of the case, the diagnosis of the specimen from Amara West was a metastatic carcinoma. Based on the dry bone alone, the origin of the cancer is very difficult to find, even impossible. The man of Amara West remains a silent witness, his skeleton serving as proof of the disease, while a voice lay hidden beneath the desert sand waiting until 1862, when a sharp-eyed collector in Luxor finally pulled cancer's origin story back into the light.

Edwin Smith was knowledgeable about hieroglyphics, but the weight of the medical terminology pulled him down. After his death in 1906, the scroll was given to the New-York Historical Society and turned over to American Egyptologist James Henry Breasted in 1920 for study. Breasted studied at Yale and Berlin before beginning to teach Egyptology at the University of Chicago. Breasted spent 10 grueling years working on the translation, and had it published in 1930.

For over 3,000 years, Imhotep’s voice had been covered under the desert sand as generations disappeared and lived the fate he had described in case 45. By the time the modern world had read case 45, they had been guessing in the dark for a cure, but a Bronze Age physician’s words still echoed. There is no treatment.

Cancer emerged the moment multicellular organisms came into existence and we’ve known and acknowledged its presence. Imhotep wrote the starting page of its biography and the rest of its story is how we fought back.
